About Costume Exchange

Made for the Stage.
Made to Be Shared.

Costume Exchange is an Australian marketplace where performers, families, clubs, schools and creative businesses buy, sell and hire new and pre-loved performance costumes.

Every year, beautiful costumes are made, worn once and packed away. Some become keepsakes. Most wait in a wardrobe for another chance to shine.

We started Costume Exchange to change that.

We believe performing should be more affordable, costumes should be enjoyed more than once, and the people who bring performances to life deserve a community that supports them.

Questions, Answered

Is it free to join Costume Exchange?

Yes. The Soloist and The Ensemble Memberships are free for individual performers and families. Platform fees still apply when you buy, sell or hire. Paid Memberships add features for clubs, schools, businesses and creative service providers.

Do I use the website or the app?

The marketplace lives in the Costume Exchange app for Apple and Android. This website introduces the community, explains the Memberships and lets you preview costume categories.

Can I buy, sell and hire on the same account?

Yes. Every member can browse and buy. Each costume you list is either for sale or for hire, not both, so choose the option that suits each item. The number of listings you can have depends on your Membership.

What are the platform fees?

Costume Exchange is free to join with The Soloist and The Ensemble Memberships. When you buy, sell or hire, it’s all done for a small platform fee, plus GST on the fee. The fee is shown in the app before you confirm.

Can I list or find team costumes?

Yes. Each listing shows whether it’s an individual or team costume, plus the number available and sizes.

How does shipping work?

Each owner sets their own delivery options. Shipping may be included in the price or quoted separately, and many owners offer local pickup. If anything is unclear, message the owner before you order.

How do hire returns and deposits work?

The hire period, return method and any deposit are agreed before the hire is confirmed. If the owner asks for a deposit, it’s taken through the platform with the hire payment and refunded when the costume comes back as agreed.

How do I contact a seller or owner?

Through secure member-to-member messaging in the app. Keep conversations on the platform so there’s a record if anything needs sorting out.

What if my item doesn’t arrive, or I want to return it?

Message the owner first. Most issues are solved quickly. If you can’t resolve it, contact us and we’ll help. Your rights under Australian Consumer Law are not affected.

How and when do I get paid?

Payout arrangements are being finalised and will be explained in the app and seller terms before you start transacting.

    The Encore Effect: Why Australia’s Costumes Deserve a Bigger Stage

    By the Costume Exchange team · 5 minute read

    Somewhere in a garage in Geelong, there’s a plastic tub of sequinned leotards. They were worn for one competition season, packed away with care, and haven’t seen a stage light since. Across town, a parent is scrolling Facebook at 11pm, hunting for exactly that costume in a size 10.

    They will probably never find each other. That’s the problem Costume Exchange was built to solve.

    Hundreds of Groups. No Way to Search Them.

    Right now, if you want to buy, sell or hire a performance costume in Australia, you have to post in a closed Facebook group. Then another one. Then another.

    There are hundreds of these groups. Each one is a small corner of the community, built around a single style, club or region. They are run by generous volunteers, and they have kept costumes moving for years. But they were never designed to be a marketplace.

    Here’s what that looks like in real life:

    • Your listing only reaches people in that group. If the right buyer isn’t a member, they will never see it.
    • Search is limited. You can’t easily filter by size, style, colour, price or whether a costume is for sale or hire.
    • Posts get buried. A listing from Tuesday can disappear under fifty new posts by Thursday.
    • Services are scattered too. Dressmakers, alterations specialists, prop makers and music editors are hard to find unless someone tags them in the comments.

    The result? Great costumes sit in storage. Performers pay full price for new ones. And talented creatives miss out on work.

    One Marketplace for the Whole Performing Arts Community

    Costume Exchange brings the whole community into one place. Dancers, calisthenics families, gymnasts, skaters, theatre companies, schools and clubs can all share their costumes with a national audience, not just the people who happen to be in the right Facebook group.

    Every costume lives in an owner’s Wardrobe in the Costume Exchange app. Buyers and renters can search and filter listings properly, check photos, sizing and condition, and message the owner directly. When a costume is hired out, any deposit is held through the platform until it comes home safely.

    It’s also a home for the people behind the scenes. The Creative Crew gives costume designers, dressmakers, lighting and set designers and other creative service providers a place to be found by performers right across Australia.

    Turning Costumes in Storage Into Income

    Performance costumes are not cheap. Many are hand-made, hand-beaded and built for a single routine. Once the season ends, most of that value sits in a cupboard.

    Costume Exchange helps turn it back into money:

    • Performers can sell or hire out costumes they have outgrown, or that no longer suit their routine.
    • Parents can recover some of what they spent, often just in time for next season’s costumes.
    • Clubs and schools can hire out past team sets and earn much-needed income for fees, travel, equipment and new costumes.

    A team set that has been sitting in a storeroom for three years could be dressing another club’s routine next month. That’s money back into the community, and a costume back on stage.

    Giving Every Costume More Life

    There’s a bigger reason this matters too. According to the Australian Fashion Council, more than 200,000 tonnes of clothing ends up in Australian landfill every year. ABC News, reporting on the council’s research, says Australians buy an average of 56 new items of clothing each year.

    Costumes are part of that story. They are often made from sequins, synthetic fabrics and trims that don’t break down easily. Every costume that finds a second, third or fourth performer is one less costume made from scratch, and one less costume headed for the tip.

    That’s the circular economy in action: keeping good things in use for as long as possible. In our world, we call it the Encore Effect.

    The Encore Effect

    A costume’s story doesn’t have to end after its first performance. A new performer. A new routine. Another moment in the spotlight.

    When costumes move freely across the whole country, everyone wins. Performing becomes more affordable. Families and clubs earn income from what they already own. Creatives find new clients. And less beautiful work ends up in landfill.

    We started Costume Exchange to unite Australia’s performing arts community and give every costume the encore it deserves.
